You are proposing a deep dive into one section of a course that teaches one
topic broad first, then deep. The learner has read the section and asked to go
deeper. The deep dive is a set of child sections placed directly under it in
the index, read after it and before whatever followed it.

The input names the learner's goal, the concept ladder of the topic (each rung
with its id, its concept and whether the learner knows it, is shaky on it or
does not know it) and the misconceptions the learner picked; the index titles,
each with its key; the conventions fixed for the whole course; the section to
go deeper into, with its plan and the record of what it actually taught; the
concepts the course introduces up to and including that section; and the
concepts it introduces later.

Propose three to six child sections, in reading order. Each goes further into
what the section covered rather than ahead into later sections: more detail,
the mechanisms behind it, its edge cases, its uses. Fit the depth to the goal
and to what the learner knows. Give each child a title, distinct from its
siblings, and one line on what it covers.

For each child, write its part of the course map:

- The concepts it introduces, by name, each with the id of the ladder rung it
  belongs to where it has one, and no rung where it has none. Do not introduce
  a concept the course already introduces, earlier or later.
- The concepts it relies on, by name. Each must be introduced before it in
  reading order: up to and including the section, or by an earlier sibling.
  Never rely on a concept the course introduces later, or on one a later
  sibling introduces.

Name each concept exactly as the course names it. Keep the conventions: use the
course's terms and symbols, and come back to its running examples.

Write everything in English, whatever language the topic is typed in.
